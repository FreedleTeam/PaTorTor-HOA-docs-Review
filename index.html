01 / Executive overview

What I would want you to know first

This home belongs to TWO associations

The Piney Creek Maintenance Association and Piney Creek Recreational Association both apply to 5566 S. Lewiston Street. This is confirmed by the two property-specific disclosure packets.

The regular 2026 total is $123/month

Maintenance: $72/month. Recreation: $51/month. Together: $1,476 per year before any one-time fees, assessments or future increases.

Recreation access is an actual benefit

The recreation association includes the outdoor pool, tennis courts, clubhouse, parks and recreational facilities. Members need access cards and must keep assessments current.

Home exterior rules still matter

The Maintenance Association’s architectural review, appearance, parking, storage and property-use restrictions continue to apply independently of your recreation access.

My perspective: This is a community with substantial shared amenities and meaningful restrictions. The bigger due-diligence questions are not just the monthly payment, but whether the association reserves adequately anticipate future repairs, whether the house has unapproved exterior work, and what will be charged at closing.

What deserves closer examination

Reserve studies are dated

Both 2025 audits cite an independent reserve study dated January 11, 2019, while the Maintenance packet also contains a later 2023 study. Buyers should request the latest actual reserve analysis for each entity and current percent-funded calculations. A bank balance alone does not tell you future adequacy.

Recreation facilities are expensive to replace

The recreation reserve list includes pool resurfacing and systems, tennis courts, lights, fencing, playgrounds, pond equipment, clubhouse roof, HVAC and interior work. These are meaningful long-term capital obligations.

Market-value fluctuations

Maintenance’s 2025 investments had an unrealized value difference of approximately $269,542 versus cost; Recreation’s were approximately $87,955 below cost. These are fund-level investment marks, not special assessments, but they warrant review of liquidity and investment policy.

Two separate assessment risks

Each association’s audit describes the ability, subject to required approvals, to raise regular assessments, levy special assessments, borrow or defer work if reserves are inadequate. Do not assume a positive audit opinion rules out future increases.

What the most recent available 2026 statements show

Through July 2026, Maintenance reserve expenses totaled approximately $232,362; examples include irrigation ($102,803) and monument work ($45,418). Recreation reserve expenses totaled approximately $207,601; the largest line was parks/playground equipment (about $123,020), with additional tennis, pool, equipment and facility spending. A 2026 expense is not automatically a budget problem—reserve spending is expected—but large projects should be checked against the updated reserve plan.

The Recreation July operating statement shows roughly $48,178 year-to-date net income; Maintenance reports about $195,311. These are interim snapshots, not full-year profits, and include timing effects from spending and assessments.

04 / Daily life

Rules that could affect how you use the property

RVs, trailers, boats and work trucks High impact

The 2016 guidelines require RVs, boats, campers, trailers, ATVs, commercial vehicles (including vehicles displaying business advertising or fitted with commercial equipment racks) to be stored inside a garage or off-site. Even where street parking is public, confirm enforcement and applicable law before relying on an exception. Vehicles cannot be parked on lawns or unpaved side yards.

Vehicle repairs

Vehicle repairs/rebuilds must occur inside the garage or outside the community. Ordinary driveway maintenance and washing taking less than a day is permitted under the guidelines.

Trash containers

Containers must be closed and stored in the garage or screened from view. Put them out no earlier than 6 p.m. the night before pickup and retrieve them on pickup day.

Pets, chickens and beehives

Normal household pets are allowed in reasonable numbers under the declaration; commercial breeding is not. Livestock, chickens/poultry and beekeeping are prohibited by the 2016 guidelines.

Home businesses

Ordinary work-from-home use is distinguishable from customer-facing business operations. The rules prohibit many businesses with recurring client visits, commercial sales, traffic or disturbance. The declaration also specifies conditions for home offices/businesses.

Outdoor storage and neighborhood appearance

Trash, sports equipment, materials and supplies may not remain in unscreened outdoor areas. Firewood must be neatly stacked and screened; lots, landscaping, fences and buildings must be kept maintained.

Noise and nuisances

Excessive dog barking, loud outdoor systems audible beyond the property and similar disturbances are restricted. Noise complaints are required to be written under the guidelines.

Decorations and signs

The 2016 guidelines call for holiday decorations to go up no earlier than 30 days before and down within 30 days after a holiday. Restrictions on real-estate, political and other signs appear in both the guidelines and declaration; applicable Colorado law may override older restrictive language, so confirm current policy.

Renting the home or using it as a short-term rental

The pages reviewed establish single-family residential use and limits on commercial activity, but do not establish a reliable current yes/no answer on leases, minimum rental terms, short-term rentals, occupancy rules or rental registration. If rental flexibility matters, get written confirmation from the HOA and check current municipal requirements before proceeding.

Packet pp. 203–207, 554–556; Community Guidelines revised April 1, 2016. Some policy language may be superseded by later rules or Colorado law.
05 / Improvements & property upkeep

Do not start an exterior project without checking ACC approval

The Architectural Control Committee (ACC) generally requires approval for exterior home work, fencing, landscaping, hardscaping and many maintenance changes. Routine submissions were described as usually requiring 7–10 business days for processing; larger projects may take longer. HOA approval does not replace city permits or engineering review.

Exterior paint

Approval is required even when repainting in an existing color. The guidelines favor earth tones; applications call for samples, a prominent test patch and neighboring color context. Unapproved painting can require repainting at the owner's expense.

Roof replacement

Materials, textures and colors are reviewed. The standards favor dimensional materials comparable to traditional cedar shake; some filings have exceptions. Verify acceptable material before ordering shingles or signing a roofing contract.

Fences

Specific approved perimeter fence types apply; chain link, barbed wire and concrete-block fencing are prohibited by the guidelines. They use a 'beneficial ownership' principle for lot-line maintenance; review property-specific fence responsibility and the separate fence drawings.

Play equipment

Trampolines, hoops, playhouses and swing sets require approval. The guidelines specify placement and height criteria; treehouses are prohibited.

Sheds, pergolas, gazebos

ACC approval is required. A general ancillary-structure guideline lists 8 × 10 feet with 10-foot peak-height guidance, compatibility with the home and a minimum four-foot property-line offset, subject to municipal setbacks and case-by-case approval. Metal/plastic sheds are not allowed by the guidelines.

Hot tubs and pools

Hot tubs/spas require ACC-approved screening and compliance with city requirements. Above-ground pools are prohibited in the 2016 guidelines.

Landscaping and drainage

Major landscape or grading changes need review. Owners must not divert water onto neighbors or interfere with drainage swales. Vegetation must be maintained; certain nuisance tree varieties and visible ornamental objects are prohibited.

Driveways, garages and utilities

Driveway alterations are heavily restricted, particularly widening at the curb. New garage doors need approval. Exterior AC and radon piping placement, windows and other visible equipment may be regulated.

For this specific house: If there is an existing fence, hot tub, shed, play structure, recent exterior paint or roof replacement, request copies of prior ACC approvals or written confirmation that no open architectural violation exists. A current feature is not automatically grandfathered or compliant.
Packet pp. 551–553, 559–568 and separate Fence Guidelines pp. 531–545 and 571–~600.
06 / Recreation and daily life

The amenities you get — and how to use them

For this house, membership in the Recreation Association is confirmed. That removes the earlier uncertainty over whether you have access to the community recreation facilities. Rules below are from the Recreation Association's May 2019 guidelines and may be updated seasonally.

Outdoor pool and wading pool

Typically open Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day. The 2019 guidelines describe typical hours around 11 a.m.–8 p.m., but the board sets actual dates and hours each season. Keycards are required. Children 12 and younger must be with an adult or responsible person at least 14. The toddler wading pool is for children 5 and under, with a responsible person over 14.

Pool guests and safety

Up to 5 guests per resident per day under the 2019 rules. No glass containers, pets (other than service animals), alcohol, tobacco, marijuana or vaping products inside the pool fencing. Pool parties and after-hours use require prior arrangements, and lifeguard charges may apply.

Eight tennis courts

Eight courts, six lighted. Access by keycard; gates generally locked 10 p.m.–6 a.m. Members reserve online via the community website for up to 2 hours, one court per day; a 15-minute late arrival can forfeit the time. Children under 12 and guests have accompaniment rules.

Clubhouse

Approximately 1,332 square feet, with gathering room and kitchen. Can be reserved by members in good standing for private functions, not commercial events. Standard window is 5–45 days in advance; a deposit and signed use agreement are required. Serving alcohol requires at least $1 million in host liquor liability insurance.

Parks, paths and playgrounds

Two playground areas, 10-acre park, shared lawns and recreation paths. No motorized vehicles on park trails, overnight camping or cooking in parks. Children under seven require an adult at playground equipment.

Pond and waterfall

Scenic features only, not a recreational pond: swimming, wading, fishing, boating and ice skating are prohibited. Children need adult supervision around the overlook and water features.

Getting access when you move in

Homeowners must be current on recreation dues to use facilities. Adult homeowners obtain access cards through the clubhouse; lost/damaged replacement cards are listed at $25. The association office is at 5800 S. Joplin Way, Centennial, CO 80015; the 2019 guide lists 303-699-8069 and mgr@pineycreek.org. Confirm the current registration procedure, guest rules, seasonal hours and program charges before relying on them.

There may be separate charges for special programs, swim team, lessons, private events or after-hours pool arrangements. These are not necessarily included in the $51 monthly recreation dues.
Recreation packet pp. 555–566; facility guide revised May 2019. Maintenance packet p. 12.
07 / Enforcement & consequences

What happens if someone violates the rules?

The declaration permits the HOA to pursue violations through a formal enforcement process, potentially including fines, cost reimbursement, legal action and collection. The enforcement policy provides notice and hearing procedures; one section specifies a written hearing request within 15 days of notice. Exact current fine schedules and legal limits should be verified against later adopted policies and applicable law.

Unpaid assessments may become liens and create additional collection costs and potentially more serious consequences. The declaration treats assessments as obligations attached to ownership and does not allow an owner to stop paying merely because they do not use common areas. Owner responsibility extends to conduct by tenants, guests and other permitted users.

Packet pp. 131–135, 190, 196–200.
